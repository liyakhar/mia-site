The third trimester: weeks 28 to 40

The last stretch brings big changes. This guide helps you get ready and know when to call.

Summary
  • Baby grows fastBaby puts on weight and gets into position for birth, often head down.
  • Know the movementsLearn your baby’s usual pattern and call the same day if it changes.
  • Get readyFinish your birth plan, pack your bag and plan the journey to your birth place.
01

What to expect

From week 28 you will probably feel more uncomfortable and tired. Heartburn, indigestion and backache are common, and your joints and ligaments are looser than usual.1

Ankles, feet and face can puff up, especially when it is hot. This is probably water retention, but get it checked in case it is pre-eclampsia, where you can feel well while your blood pressure rises dangerously high very quickly.1

If you are worried about anything, talk to your midwife or doctor, or call NHS 111.1

02

Vaccines

The RSV vaccine is recommended at around week 28, and can be given later, right up until labour. It can lower the risk of severe bronchiolitis in your baby’s first 6 months by about 70%.1

If you have not had the whooping cough vaccine, you can have it up to week 32. Speak to your midwife or GP.1

03

Your baby’s movements

Keep getting to know your baby’s usual pattern of movements. Call your midwife or maternity unit straight away if they move less than usual or the pattern changes.2

Do not wait until the next day, even in the middle of the night.2

04

Sleep

Sleep on either side rather than on your back. After week 28, sleeping on your back may double the risk of stillbirth.3

05

Check-ups

At 36 weeks your midwife checks your blood pressure, urine and bump, and feels the position of your baby. These appointments can pick up changes you may not notice, such as very high blood pressure.45

If your baby is not head down by 36 weeks you may be offered a turn, called ECV. It works about half the time.4

At 38 and 40 weeks your midwife talks about your choices if you are still pregnant after 40 weeks.5

06

Planning the birth

In England about 6 in 10 births are vaginal births without help, nearly 3 in 10 are caesarean births and just over 1 in 10 are assisted with ventouse or forceps.4

If you have had a caesarean before, a vaginal birth is often still possible. It depends on why you needed one. Talk it through with your midwife or doctor.4

Around week 40, when contractions last at least 60 seconds and come every 5 minutes, call your hospital or midwife.6

Call
Call your midwife if
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • Swelling of your face, hands or feet that comes on quickly
  • Contractions lasting at least 60 seconds, every 5 minutes
  • Your waters break, or you have vaginal bleeding
  • You are worried about anything
Good to know
Is it normal to feel more tired again?

Yes. The third trimester often brings heartburn, backache and tiredness. Rest when you can, and call your midwife if you are worried about anything.

Can I bring on labour at home?

There are no proven ways to safely bring on labour at home. Ask your doctor or midwife before trying anything other than watching and waiting.
