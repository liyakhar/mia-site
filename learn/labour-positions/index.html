Best positions for labour and birth

How you move in labour can change how it feels. These are simple positions to try, and why they help.

Summary
  • Stay uprightStanding, walking and leaning on a partner use gravity and can help baby move down.
  • Use a birth ballSitting and rocking on a ball can ease backache and keep your hips loose.
  • Hands and kneesKneeling on all fours can help if you have backache.
  • Change oftenMoving and changing position can help. Do what feels right for you.
01

Why move

On TV people give birth lying on their backs. That is not always the best position and may slow labour down.1

Moving around, staying upright and changing position can increase your chance of a shorter labour, help you feel less pain, lead to fewer interventions including a lower chance of caesarean birth, and make you feel more in control.1

Most people feel a need to move rather than keep still. Try to walk around in the first stage if you can.12

02

Positions to try

Sit leaning on a table, straddle a chair or toilet facing the back, or stand leaning on a bed, a table or your birth partner.1

Kneel on the floor cradling a birth ball, or kneel on all fours. All fours can help if you have backache.1

Rock, sit or gently bounce on a birth ball. Try a lunge with one knee bent, standing, kneeling or lying on your side. Squat if you have practised it.1

If you get tired or contractions get stronger, shift your weight from foot to foot or rock your pelvis. Rest when you need to.1

03

In the pushing stage

In the second stage you may want to sit, lie on your side, stand, kneel or squat, though squatting may be hard if you are not used to it. Try some of these before labour.2

Finding a comfortable upright position can help shorten the time you push for, and reduce your chance of an episiotomy and assisted birth.1

If you had a lot of backache in labour, kneeling on all fours may help.2

04

Touch and water

Massage and soothing touch can help release oxytocin, the hormone that helps labour progress, and endorphins, your natural pain relief.1

A birth pool can make changing position easier. Labouring in water is only advised if you and your baby are healthy and your pregnancy is low risk. If you have had opioid pain relief such as diamorphine, wait for it to wear off before getting in.1

05

If you have an epidural

An epidural can limit movement, partly because of your legs but mostly because of closer monitoring and the drip. Your midwife should still help you into comfortable upright positions in the first stage if you can manage it.1

It also raises the chance of needing help to give birth, though less with modern epidurals. If it is your first baby and you find it hard to stay upright, lying on your side in the second stage may help avoid an assisted birth.1

Good to know
What is the best position for labour?

There is no single best one. Do what feels right and keep changing. Upright and moving positions are generally helpful.

Can I move with an epidural?

Movement is more limited, but your midwife should still help you find comfortable upright positions in the first stage if you can manage it.
