Safe sleep for newborns

Safe sleep comes down to a short list. Learn it once and you can rest more easily.

Summary
  • On the backAlways put baby to sleep on their back, for every sleep.
  • Own spaceA firm, flat mattress in a cot or Moses basket in your room for the first six months.
  • Keep it clearNo pillows, bumpers, loose blankets or soft toys in the sleep space.
  • Avoid riskDo not sleep with baby on a sofa or armchair, and do not smoke around baby.
01

What SIDS is

Sudden infant death syndrome, or SIDS, is the sudden and unexplained death of an apparently healthy baby up to 12 months old. It used to be called cot death.1

It is rare, and no one knows what causes it. But some things raise the risk, such as cigarette smoke and sleeping with your baby on a sofa or chair. Babies born before 37 weeks or weighing under 2.5 kg are at higher risk.1

It is most likely in the first 6 months and can happen any time a baby is asleep, day or night. If your baby was born early, follow safer sleep advice for a year from the due date, not the birth date.1

02

Do

Always place your baby on their back to sleep, with their feet at the bottom of the cot or Moses basket. If they roll, move them onto their back until they can roll both ways themselves.1

Use a separate cot or Moses basket in the same room as you for every sleep for at least the first 6 months. Use a firm, flat mattress.1

Keep the cot clear. No pillows, duvets, bumpers, loose blankets, soft toys, pods or nests.1

Breastfeed if you can. Think about a dummy to settle your baby to sleep, as some research suggests it may help lower the risk. Keep your baby’s routine vaccinations up to date.1

03

Don’t

Do not sleep on a sofa or in an armchair with your baby. Do not smoke when pregnant or around your baby, and do not let anyone smoke in the same room.1

Do not let your baby stay in a car seat too long. It is fine to fall asleep while travelling, but take them out as soon as you arrive.1

04

Not too hot, not too cold

Keep the room between about 16 and 20°C and check with a thermometer. Overheating can raise the risk.1

Feel your baby’s chest or the back of their neck. Cool hands and feet are normal. Remove layers if they are hot or sweating. Use lightweight blankets, sheets or a baby sleeping bag.1

Never use a hot water bottle or electric blanket, or put your baby next to a radiator, heater or fire or in direct sunlight.1

05

If you share a bed

The safest place is a separate cot or Moses basket. If you do share a bed, use a firm flat mattress, keep pillows and blankets away, do not swaddle, keep other children and pets out, and make sure your baby cannot fall out or get trapped.1

Do not share a bed if your baby was born before 37 weeks or weighed under 2.5 kg. Also do not if you or your partner smoke, have had 2 or more units of alcohol, taken recreational drugs, or taken medicine that makes you drowsy.1

Good to know
Is it safe to sleep with my baby on the sofa?

No. Sleeping on a sofa or armchair with your baby raises the risk of SIDS.

What temperature should the room be?

About 16 to 20°C. Feel your baby’s chest or back of the neck, and remove layers if they feel hot or sweaty.
