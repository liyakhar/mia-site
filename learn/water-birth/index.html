Water birth: what the research says

Warm water can help you relax and move freely in labour, and many birth centres and hospitals have a pool. This guide covers what the research found and how to ask for one.

Summary
  • Why water helpsWarm water supports your weight and can ease pain. Some people need less other pain relief.
  • What the research foundResearch suggests labour in water is safe with a low-risk pregnancy. Babies’ health looks similar to birth out of water.
  • Who it suitsUsually people with a healthy, low-risk pregnancy. Your midwife will tell you if it suits you.
  • Plan aheadAsk early if your birth place has a pool, and what happens if it is in use.
01

What a water birth is

A water birth means labouring or giving birth in a deep bath or birthing pool.1

Water can help with pain and many people find it more relaxing. It supports your weight so you can move more easily and feel more in control.1

02

Who can have one

It is an option if your pregnancy is low risk and your midwife or doctor thinks it is safe for you and your baby. Tell your midwife early.1

It is generally not advised if your baby is breech, you are having twins or triplets, your baby is under 37 weeks or has passed meconium, or you have an infection, a high temperature or bleeding. It is also not advised if you have pre-eclampsia, your waters broke over 24 hours ago, you are on an induction drip, or you are at high risk of birth difficulties. Local guidance may differ.1

The reason is that it may be hard to get you out of the pool safely in an emergency.1

03

Is it safe

Research suggests labour in water is safe if you have a low-risk pregnancy and can use a pool. Newborn infection and umbilical cord tears after water birth are very rare and can usually be treated.1

There is not enough research to say whether water birth is safer or less safe than birth out of water. So far, babies’ health, including the chance of infection, looks similar.1

Drowning is very unlikely. A baby born in water behaves as if still in the womb until they take their first breath of air.1

04

The advantages

Warm water can relax, soothe and comfort you. You can try different positions, and gravity helps your baby move down when you are upright.12

It can lower your blood pressure and anxiety, help back pain and ease the feeling of pressure when you are fully dilated.1

05

The downsides

You cannot have an epidural, and you cannot have opiates such as pethidine for about 2 to 3 hours before getting in. You cannot use a TENS machine in the pool because of the danger of electrocution.1

The effect on your baby’s temperature is unclear, so your midwife checks the water often. It is uncertain whether water affects the risk or severity of perineal tears. You may need to leave the pool if there is a complication.1

A pool may not be free if your unit is busy. The NHS says the water is kept at a comfortable temperature, not above 37.5°C.13

Good to know
Can I change my mind about a water birth?

Yes. You can change your mind at any time, in or out of the water.

Can I have an epidural in the pool?

No. You also cannot have opiates like pethidine for 2 to 3 hours before getting in.
