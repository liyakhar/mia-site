Breathing and relaxation for labour

Slow, steady breathing can help you cope with contractions. You can practise these techniques at home in a few minutes a day.

Summary
  • Slow breathingBreathe in through your nose and out through your mouth. It may help you feel more in control.
  • RelaxationSoften your jaw, shoulders and hands. A relaxed body often means a calmer mind.
  • Practise oftenA few minutes a day in pregnancy makes it easier to use when labour comes.
  • The evidenceSome research shows hypnobirthing can reduce fear and pain, though not all studies agree. You can use it with other pain relief.
01

What it is

Hypnobirthing is a mix of visualisation, relaxation and deep breathing. The aim is to help you feel calmer, more in control and better able to cope with labour pain.1

You can use it with any other pain relief, and add it to your birth plan.1

02

The techniques

Controlled breathing: in through your nose and out through your mouth. It may help you feel more in control and cope without medical pain relief.1

Visualisation: picture something pleasant, such as a flower opening with each wave, or holding your baby skin to skin.1

Deep relaxation: a kind of meditation that helps you focus on your body and baby and block out noise around you.1

Positive words, such as “each surge brings me closer to meeting my baby”, can help too.1

03

Does it work

Tommy’s says research has shown hypnobirthing can reduce fear and pain and give you more sense of control. It may help anxiety and reduce medical interventions, though not all studies have found this.1

The NHS says most complementary methods, including hypnosis, are not proven to give effective pain relief. If you want to try them, talk to your midwife first.2

Practising it does not mean labour will go smoothly. Learning to stay calm may still help if things do not go to plan.1

04

How to practise

Some people practise a few times a day, others 2 to 3 times a week. Fit it in while you are pregnant so it works for you in labour.1

You can learn from a class, a book, an app or free online courses. In some areas NHS midwives run classes. Most private courses cost money.1

05

One thing to tell your midwife

Hypnobirthing can be so relaxing that a midwife may think you are at an earlier stage than you are. Say clearly that you are using it when you phone or arrive.1

Good to know
Is hypnobirthing proven to work?

Tommy’s says research shows it can reduce fear and pain, though not all studies agree. The NHS says most complementary methods are not proven for pain. It does not replace medical pain relief if you want it.

Can I use it with an epidural or gas and air?

Yes. You can use hypnobirthing techniques alongside any other pain relief.
