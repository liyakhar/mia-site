What a doula does, and whether you need one

A doula is a trained birth companion. They give no medical care. They stay with you, help you feel steady and help you speak up for what you want.

Summary
  • Before birthTalks through your wishes, your fears and your birth plan with you.
  • In labourStays with you the whole time. Helps with positions, breathing, touch and calm.
  • After birthSome doulas visit in the first weeks to help with feeding and rest.
  • What the research foundPeople with continuous support in labour are less likely to have an assisted birth, a caesarean or a longer labour.
01

What a doula is

A doula supports you one-to-one through pregnancy, labour and birth, or after your baby arrives. They offer practical and emotional support wherever and however you give birth.1

They do not have to be medically trained and cannot replace the clinical care of your midwife or doctor. They give extra support and can help you find balanced information.1

02

What they can do

Birth doulas meet you in pregnancy, help with your birth plan, answer questions, and support you in labour whether you plan a caesarean, home birth or water birth.1

Postnatal doulas help with practical and emotional support, confidence in caring for your baby and breastfeeding.1

03

Does it help

People who have continuous support in labour and birth are less likely to have an assisted birth, a caesarean, pain relief or a longer labour.1

There is also some evidence that doula support improves the birth experience and your baby’s wellbeing just after birth.1

A doula can speak up for you, which may help if you are anxious or have cultural or religious needs. They may also help if you are Black or Black Mixed Heritage and worry about not being listened to.1

04

Cost and training

Costs vary a lot, by where they work, their experience and what is included. Ask each doula directly.1

You may be able to get free support through charities. Doula UK helps people who are disadvantaged or vulnerable, and you need to be referred by a healthcare professional or social worker.1

There is no national regulation. Not all doulas are insured and some have no training, so ask about both.1

05

Finding one

NCT doulas have completed a 9-month course and have professional insurance. Doula UK members have completed an approved preparation course. You can also ask your midwife about local doulas.1

Many doulas offer a first meeting, so you can see whether you are a good fit.1

Good to know
Is a doula a replacement for my midwife?

No. A doula gives extra support but cannot replace the clinical care of your midwife or doctor.

Can I get a doula for free?

Sometimes. Doula UK and some local charities support people who are disadvantaged or vulnerable. You usually need a referral.
