How to write a birth plan

A birth plan is a short note about what matters to you in labour. Keep it to one page that your team can read in a minute.

Summary

  • Keep it short. One page is enough. Use plain words and put your most important wishes first.
  • What to include. Who you want with you, positions and movement, pain relief wishes, and your feeding plan.
  • Plan for change. Birth can go a different way. Add what you would want if plans change, such as skin-to-skin after a caesarean.
  • Share it early. Talk it through with your midwife before labour and bring a copy.
Call your midwife if
  • If you are unsure about any part of your plan, ask your midwife at your next check-up

What a birth plan is

A birth plan is a short note of what matters to you in labour and after the birth. It helps your team get to know you quickly.

What to include

Who you want with you. Where you want to give birth. How you want to cope: movement, water, breathing or pain relief.

What happens after: skin-to-skin, delayed cord clamping, how you want to feed, and vitamin K for your baby.

Plan for changes

Add a short section for if plans change, for example if you need induction, an assisted birth or a caesarean. What would still matter to you?

How to write it

Keep it to one page. Use bullet points, and put the most important things first.

Talk it through with your midwife around weeks 34 to 36, and bring copies to your birth place.

Good to know

Is a birth plan a promise?

No. It is a wish list. Your team will keep you and your baby safe first.

Who should see my birth plan?

Your midwife, your birth partner and anyone supporting you in labour.

When should I write it?

Many people start around week 32 and finish by week 36.
