Postnatal recovery: body and mind

Your body has done something huge. Give yourself time to rest and heal, and know the signs that need a call.

Summary

  • Bleeding. Bleeding for a few weeks is normal. Call if it is very heavy, smells bad or you pass large clots.
  • Stitches and soreness. Keep clean and dry, rest, and use pain relief as advised. Call if pain gets worse.
  • Pelvic floor. Pelvic floor exercises can help healing. A midwife can show you how.
  • Rest and mood. Sleep when you can and accept help. Low mood that lasts more than two weeks needs a talk with your doctor.
Call your midwife if
  • Sudden, very heavy bleeding, or you feel faint or dizzy. Call 999
  • Temperature over 38°C, or bleeding that smells unusual
  • Wound or stitches more painful, swollen or leaking pus
  • Feeling low, hopeless or very anxious for more than 2 weeks

The first weeks

You will bleed from your vagina for a few weeks. It usually stops after 6 to 8 weeks, but can last longer.NHS

Some people feel tummy pain as the womb shrinks, especially when breastfeeding. This is normal.NHS

Midwives visit you at home or in a clinic until your baby is about 10 days old. A health visitor sees you around 10 to 14 days.NHS

Stitches from a tear or episiotomy should heal within about a month. A caesarean takes longer to recover from, and you may need to avoid driving, exercise, heavy lifting and sex for about 6 weeks.NHSNHS

Moving your body

Gentle exercise helps your body recover and can help you feel more energetic. Walking, gentle stretches, and pelvic floor and tummy exercises are good places to start.NHS

Even a short daily walk with your baby can help you feel less tired.NHS

Sleep and tiredness

Looking after a baby is tiring, especially in the first months when your baby wakes several times a night. This phase does not last forever. As babies get older they sleep for longer.NHS

Rest when your baby sleeps, even if it means leaving the housework. Share the nights if you can. A partner can do nappies or morning dressing, or give a bottle of expressed milk once breastfeeding is settled.NHS

Ask friends or relatives to watch your baby while you nap. If you are on your own, see if someone can stay a few days.NHS

If you cannot sleep even when your baby sleeps, or feel tired all the time, these can be signs of postnatal depression. Talk to a GP or health visitor.NHS

Your 6-week check

You should have a postnatal check 6 to 8 weeks after the birth. You can request one yourself, especially if you have worries. Make a list of questions to take.NHS

It usually covers how you are feeling, vaginal discharge and periods, blood pressure if you had problems, an examination if you had stitches or ongoing pain, contraception, and how feeding is going.NHS

Tell your doctor if you feel sad or anxious, have trouble holding in pee or wind, have pain during sex or in the area between your vagina and bottom, or are unsure about your vaccines.NHS

Contraception

You can have sex as soon as you feel ready. There are no rules. But you can get pregnant from 21 days after birth, even before a period and even if you are breastfeeding.NHS

If you do not want to get pregnant, use contraception from 21 days. If you have had a tear or episiotomy, pain during sex is common in the first few months.NHSNHS

Good to know

When is my postnatal check?

Between 6 and 8 weeks after the birth. You can request one yourself if you have concerns.

How long does bleeding last?

Usually 6 to 8 weeks, but it can last longer. Speak to your midwife, GP or health visitor if you are worried.
