Pregnancy week by week

Each week brings small changes for you and your baby. Mia gives you one clear view of your week: how big your baby is, what your body is doing and one thing worth doing now.

Summary

  • Weeks 1 to 12. The first trimester. Tiredness and nausea are common. Your dating scan is offered at 11 to 14 weeks.
  • Weeks 13 to 27. The second trimester. Many people feel more energy. You may feel the first movements.
  • Weeks 28 to 40. The third trimester. Get to know your baby’s usual pattern of movements and call if it changes.
  • After 40 weeks. Many babies arrive after their due date. Your midwife will talk with you about your options.
Call your midwife if
  • You are worried about your health or your baby’s
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • Swelling of your face, hands or feet that comes on quickly. It may be pre-eclampsia
  • Contractions lasting 60 seconds and coming every 5 minutes

How pregnancy is dated

Pregnancy is dated from the first day of your last period.NHS

You are in your first trimester up to week 12, your second from week 13, and your third from week 28.NHS

The first trimester

In week 4 your baby is about the size of a poppy seed. By week 12 they are about the size of a plum, and many people start to feel better as the first trimester ends.NHSNHS

Early on you may have no symptoms at all. Common ones are a missed period, nausea, tiredness, sore breasts and needing to pee more often.NHS

The second trimester

By week 20 you are halfway. You may have your anomaly scan around now, and your hands, feet and gums may swell a little.NHS

You are offered the whooping cough vaccine, usually around week 20 but any time from week 16 to 32. The protection passes to your baby through the placenta.NHS

The third trimester

From week 28 you will probably feel more tired and uncomfortable. Heartburn, backache and swollen ankles are common.NHS

The RSV vaccine is recommended from around week 28. It can lower your baby’s risk of severe bronchiolitis in their first 6 months.NHS

By week 40 you may get a lot of practice contractions. When contractions last at least 60 seconds and come every 5 minutes, call your hospital or midwife.NHS

Your check-ups

If this is your first baby you will be offered at least 10 antenatal appointments and 2 scans. If you have had a baby before, at least 7.NHS

Your first midwife appointment is usually at 8 to 12 weeks. At each visit your midwife checks your blood pressure and urine, and later measures your bump and talks about your baby’s movements.NHS

You can contact maternity services at any time if you are worried. You do not have to wait for your next appointment.NHS

Good to know

How many weeks is a full pregnancy?

Most pregnancies are counted as around 40 weeks from the first day of your last period. Your midwife will talk through your due date and your options if you go past it.

Do I have to wait for my next appointment if I am worried?

No. You can contact maternity services at any time, day or night.
