Postnatal depression: signs and where to get help

Many new parents feel low. Mia explains the difference between baby blues and depression and how to get help fast.

Summary

  • Baby blues. Tearfulness and mood swings in the first two weeks are common and pass.
  • Postnatal depression. If low mood, anxiety or feeling unable to cope lasts more than two weeks, speak to your midwife, health visitor or GP.
  • Partners too. Partners can also feel low. Help is for them as well.
  • Urgent help. If you have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, call your GP, 111 or 999 now.
Call your midwife if
  • You feel low, hopeless or very anxious for more than 2 weeks. See your GP, midwife or health visitor
  • You have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby. Get urgent help now. Call 999 or Samaritans on 116 123
  • You cannot sleep even when your baby sleeps

What it is

Postnatal depression is a common mental health condition that can happen after having a baby. It usually gets better with treatment.NHS

It can begin while you are pregnant, soon after birth, or up to a year after. Fathers and partners can have it too.NHS

The signs

Low mood, finding it hard to enjoy anything, feeling hopeless or that you cannot cope, and feeling guilty, worthless or like a bad parent.NHS

Feeling fearful, anxious or worrying all the time, restless or irritable, with trouble sleeping even when you have time to rest.NHS

Difficulty concentrating, difficult feelings about your baby or trouble bonding, and thoughts of suicide or harming yourself or your baby.NHS

Baby blues or something more

Feeling down, anxious or irritable for a few days after birth is very common. It is called the baby blues and usually goes within 2 weeks.NHS

If it continues, gets worse or you are struggling to cope, get help. It is important to get help even if you only have some of the signs.NHS

Getting help

Speak to your GP, midwife or health visitor. You can also refer yourself to NHS talking therapies if you are 18 or over, with no GP referral needed.NHS

Tell your doctor at your 6-week check if you feel sad or anxious. You do not need to struggle alone or put on a brave face, and it does not mean you are a bad parent.NHS

You may fear your baby will be taken away if you ask for help. This is very rare. Your care team will support you to care for your baby.NHS

Treatment

Treatment includes talking therapies such as CBT and antidepressants. Many antidepressants are safe while breastfeeding. Tell your doctor you are breastfeeding and they will choose a suitable one.NHS

It usually gets better in 3 to 6 months, though it can last longer. It is more likely to get better with treatment.NHS

What you can do

Talk about how you feel with a friend, family member, health professional or counsellor. You can call Samaritans on 116 123. Ask for help with shopping, chores and childcare.NHS

Get regular exercise, even a short walk, and go to a local support group or parent and baby group. Rest and sleep as much as you can, and make time for things you enjoy.NHS

Do not use illegal drugs or alcohol to manage your symptoms.NHS

Good to know

Is it the baby blues or postnatal depression?

The baby blues usually go within 2 weeks of the birth. If low feelings continue, get worse, or you are struggling to cope, talk to your GP, midwife or health visitor.

Will my baby be taken away if I ask for help?

This is very rare. Your care team will give you the support you need to care for your baby.

Can partners get it?

Yes. Fathers and partners can also have depression after having a baby.
