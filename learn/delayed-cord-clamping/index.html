Delayed cord clamping and the golden hour

The first minutes after birth matter. This guide explains cord clamping in plain words.

Summary
  • Waiting helpsWaiting at least one minute before clamping lets more blood pass to baby and is advised for most births.
  • Skin-to-skinHolding baby on your chest keeps them warm and settled and helps feeding start.
  • At the birthYou can still cut the cord yourself. Delayed clamping is also possible after a caesarean.
  • If baby needs helpSometimes the cord is cut sooner so baby can get care. Your team will tell you why.
01

What it is

Delayed or optimal cord clamping means your midwife or doctor waits 1 to 5 minutes before cutting the cord. The cord should not be clamped earlier than 1 minute after birth.1

For those minutes the cord keeps passing blood, oxygen and stem cells to your baby while they adjust to breathing air.1

02

Why it matters

Your baby gets a full volume of blood at birth. This gives higher iron levels until about 6 months, which helps growth and development.1

It also gives more stem cells, which help growth and the immune system. There is good evidence that delayed clamping can prevent deaths in premature babies.1

03

What it looks like

The NHS suggests your baby is lifted onto you straight after birth, before the cord is cut, so you are close right away.2

You or your birth partner can still cut the cord. Delayed clamping can also happen if you have help delivering the placenta, or if you have a caesarean, planned or emergency.1

Delayed clamping should happen automatically, but it can help to mention it in your birth plan.3

04

When it may not happen

It is almost always recommended. Your team may clamp earlier if your baby needs to be treated quickly, for example for their heartbeat or breathing.1

Rare reasons include heavy bleeding in the mother, a problem with the placenta such as placental abruption or placenta praevia, or a bleeding cord. Twins who share a placenta should not have delayed clamping.1

If your baby needs help breathing, your team may be able to help without cutting the cord. Some units have equipment to do this.1

Good to know
How long is delayed cord clamping?

Between 1 and 5 minutes after birth. The cord should not be clamped before 1 minute.

Can I have it with a caesarean?

Yes. Giving birth by caesarean does not in itself prevent delayed cord clamping.
