Caesarean birth: planned and unplanned

A caesarean is a real birth. Here is why it may be advised, what happens, and how you can still make it feel like yours.

Why a caesarean happens

A caesarean is an operation to deliver your baby through a cut in your tummy and womb. About four in ten births in England are now caesareans.

It can be planned, for example for a breech baby or placenta problems, or happen in labour if needed. You can also ask for one.

What happens

You are usually awake with a spinal or epidural, so you feel no pain, only pulling. A screen hides the operation, and your birth partner can sit by your head.

Your baby is born in about 10 minutes. The full operation takes around 40 minutes.

Making it yours

Ask for skin-to-skin in theatre, delayed cord clamping, a lowered screen to see your baby born, and your own music.

Recovery

Expect to stay in hospital one to two days. Walking soon after helps healing.

Avoid heavy lifting for about six weeks. Ask before driving.

Future births

Many people can have a vaginal birth after a caesarean (VBAC). About three in four who try succeed.

When to call your midwife

  • Wound red, hot, leaking or smelly
  • Fever
  • Heavy bleeding
  • Pain or swelling in one leg
  • Breathlessness or chest pain. Call 999

Maternity units are open day and night. Calling is never a waste of anyone’s time.

Based on NICE caesarean birth guideline and NHS guidance. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Sources: NICE guideline NG192: Caesarean birth · NHS: Caesarean section · RCOG: Birth after previous caesarean

Good to know

Can I have skin-to-skin after a caesarean?

Often, yes. Ask your team before the birth.

Can I have a vaginal birth after a caesarean?

Many people can. Talk to your midwife or doctor early in pregnancy.

How long is recovery?

Around six weeks for most people.
