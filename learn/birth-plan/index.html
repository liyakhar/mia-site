How to write a birth plan

A birth plan is a short note about what matters to you in labour. Keep it to one page that your team can read in a minute.

Start free
01

Keep it short

One page is enough. Use plain words and put your most important wishes first.

02

What to include

Who you want with you, positions and movement, pain relief wishes, and your feeding plan.

03

Plan for change

Birth can go a different way. Add what you would want if plans change, such as skin-to-skin after a caesarean.

04

Share it early

Talk it through with your midwife before labour and bring a copy.

Based on NHS and NICE guidance on shared decision making in maternity care. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Good to know

Is a birth plan a promise?

No. It is a wish list. Your team will keep you and your baby safe first.

Who should see my birth plan?

Your midwife, your birth partner and anyone supporting you in labour.
