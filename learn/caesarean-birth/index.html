Caesarean birth: planned and unplanned

A caesarean is a real birth. Here is why it may be advised, what happens, and how you can still make it feel like yours.

Start free
01

Why it happens

Reasons include baby’s position, a health concern or labour not progressing. Some are planned and some are not.

02

What happens

You are usually awake with a spinal block. The operation is quick, and your birth partner can often be there.

03

Make it yours

Ask about skin-to-skin in theatre, delayed cord clamping and your feeding plan.

04

Recovery

Rest, gentle walking and pain relief help. Call your midwife about a fever, a wound that looks red or smells, or heavy bleeding.

Based on NICE caesarean birth guideline and NHS guidance. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Good to know

Can I have skin-to-skin after a caesarean?

Often, yes. Ask your team before the birth.

Can I have a vaginal birth after a caesarean?

Many people can. Talk to your midwife or doctor early in pregnancy.
