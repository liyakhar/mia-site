Induction of labour: what to expect

Induction means starting labour with medical help. Mia explains why it is offered, how it works and the questions worth asking.

Why it is offered

Common reasons are going past your due date, your waters breaking without labour, or a health concern for you or baby.

How it works

It can start with a gel or pessary, a balloon, a membrane sweep or a hormone drip, step by step.

It can take time

Induction often takes longer than labour that starts on its own. Bring things to pass the time.

Ask questions

Ask what the risks are of both inducing and waiting, and what your other choices are.

Based on NICE guideline on inducing labour and NHS guidance. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Good to know

Can I say no to induction?

Yes. It is your decision. Your team will explain the risks and benefits so you can choose.

Does a membrane sweep hurt?

It can feel uncomfortable or crampy for a short time.
