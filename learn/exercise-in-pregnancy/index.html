Exercise in pregnancy: what is safe

Moving in pregnancy is good for you and your baby. Mia shows what is safe, what to avoid and when to stop.

Summary

  • Keep moving. About 150 minutes of moderate activity a week is advised if you are well. Walking and swimming are easy places to start.
  • Strength and stretching. Gentle strength work, yoga and pelvic floor exercises are fine for most people. Tell the teacher you are pregnant.
  • What to avoid. Avoid contact sports, activities with a risk of falling, and lying flat on your back for long periods later on.
  • Stop and call if. You feel faint, overheated or in any pain. Call your midwife about bleeding or less baby movement.
Call your midwife if
  • You feel dizzy, faint or very unwell while exercising
  • You have bleeding, fluid leaking or tummy pain
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • You have a health condition and are not sure what is safe

Is it safe

Exercise is safe for you and your baby in pregnancy. Start any new exercise gently. It is usually fine to keep doing what you already do.NHS

Being active can keep you fit, prepare your body for birth, help your recovery, improve sleep, mood and mental health, and lower the risk of some conditions like gestational diabetes and pre-eclampsia.NHS

How much

Aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, the same advice as for all adults. You should be able to talk but not sing.NHS

You do not need to do it all at once. Shorter sessions every day work too. Try to include 2 sessions a week of muscle strengthening, such as yoga, carrying shopping or strength training.NHS

If you cannot reach 150 minutes, do what you can. Any activity is better than none.NHS

Getting started

If you are new to exercise, start with about 10 minutes a day and build up slowly.NHS

It is not recommended to start a new vigorous activity such as running in pregnancy. If you have a health condition, ask your midwife what is safe for you.NHS

Walking, swimming and yoga are good options. Swimming eases pressure on your muscles and joints.NHSTommy’s

Staying safe

Tell your instructor you are pregnant. Drink water, keep cool, and stop if you feel uncomfortable, too hot or unwell.NHS

Avoid sports with a risk of falling or bumping your tummy, such as hockey, rugby or mountain biking. Tommy’s also lists boxing, football, horse riding and climbing. Do not scuba dive, and do not exercise on an empty stomach.NHSTommy’s

In later pregnancy, avoid lying flat on your back for more than a few minutes. It can make you feel dizzy and is not good for your baby.NHS

Trimester by trimester

First trimester: if you were active before, carry on. If you are new to it, start gently. Do not exercise in very hot conditions, and drink plenty of fluids. Start your pelvic floor exercises.Tommy’s

Second trimester: think about swapping high-impact exercise like running and aerobics for walking or swimming. If you ran regularly it is fine to carry on for as long as you feel comfortable. Do not lie on your back for longer than a few minutes.Tommy’s

Third trimester: you can stay active right up to the birth if you feel well. Keep to low-impact activities such as walking, swimming and dancing, and slow down as your due date gets closer.Tommy’s

Worries

There is no evidence that physical activity or exercise causes miscarriage or premature birth. Your baby is well protected and cushioned inside the womb. If you are at risk of complications such as premature labour, take extra care and ask your midwife.Tommy’s

The UK Chief Medical Officers recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week. Stop straight away if you feel faint, overheated or in any pain.Tommy’s

Listen to your body

As your bump grows you may find other activities more comfortable. If you used to run, brisk walking may feel better.NHS

Do not push yourself too hard. If something does not feel right, stop and talk to your midwife.NHS

Good to know

How much exercise should I do?

Aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, including muscle strengthening. Do what you can. Any activity is better than none.

Can I keep running?

It is usually fine to keep doing activities you already do. It is not recommended to start a new vigorous activity in pregnancy.

What should I avoid?

Contact sports and activities with a risk of falling, scuba diving, exercising on an empty stomach, and lying flat on your back for long in later pregnancy.
