Do dates help labour? What the research says

Many people hear that eating dates near the end of pregnancy helps labour. This guide covers what the studies found and how strong the proof is.

Summary
  • What people doSome people eat dates in the weeks before their due date, as a snack.
  • What studies foundThere is some evidence dates may help, but bigger and better studies are needed.
  • Check firstThere are no proven ways to safely start labour at home. Ask your midwife before trying anything.
  • Keep it simpleDates are a food, not a treatment. They do not replace care from your midwife.
01

Waiting past 40 weeks

Many babies arrive after their due date. At your 38 and 40 week appointments your midwife talks through your choices if you are still pregnant after 40 weeks.1

If labour has not started by 41 weeks, induction is offered because the risk of stillbirth or problems for the baby is higher.2

02

The truth about home remedies

There is not enough strong evidence that home remedies start labour, and some may not be safe. Always ask your midwife first.3

The NHS says there are no proven ways of starting labour yourself at home. There is also no safety information on herbal supplements.2

03

Dates

There is a theory that eating dates for a few weeks before your due date may help your cervix open. Dates contain prostaglandins, which may play a part in starting labour.3

There is some evidence they can help, but bigger and better studies are needed to be sure. A handful of dates is a healthy snack either way.3

04

Walking and other ideas

There is a small amount of evidence that walking for 30 minutes, three times a week, may increase your chance of going into labour. Walk only as far and as fast as is comfortable.3

There is little to suggest acupuncture can trigger labour. If you try it, use a fully qualified practitioner who is used to pregnancy.3

There is no proof that spicy food works, and no evidence that sex starts labour. Sex will not cause harm, but avoid it if your waters have broken.23

05

Raspberry leaf tea

Health professionals do not suggest raspberry leaf tea for getting labour going. Experts are unsure whether it works or how safe it is.3

It may not be safe if you have had a caesarean in the last 2 years or plan one, have high blood pressure or pre-eclampsia, had premature labour before, are carrying more than one baby or a breech baby, or take antidepressants. Check with your midwife first.3

06

What your midwife can offer

From 39 weeks you can be offered a membrane sweep to bring on labour. If your pregnancy goes past 42 weeks and you decide against induction, you should be offered more monitoring.2

Use the waiting time to rest and do things you enjoy.3

Call
Call your midwife if
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • Your waters break
  • Vaginal bleeding
  • You are past your due date and want to talk through your options
Good to know
Do dates really bring on labour?

There is some evidence they may help, but bigger studies are needed. They are a healthy snack, but not a proven way to start labour.

Is raspberry leaf tea safe?

Experts are unsure. Talk to your midwife before trying it, as it is not safe in some situations.
