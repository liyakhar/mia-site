What a doula does

A doula is a trained birth companion. They give emotional and practical support in pregnancy, in labour and often in the early weeks after birth.

A doula is not a midwife and does not do medical tasks. They stay with you, help you cope and help you feel heard.

What the research says

A large Cochrane review found that continuous support in labour, especially from someone who is not a hospital staff member, is linked with more births without instruments or surgery, shorter labours and less need for pain relief.

People who had continuous support were also more likely to feel good about their birth.

How a doula helps in labour

Massage, positions, breathing, reminding you of your wishes and calm words. They also support your partner, so they can rest and stay close.

Finding and paying for a doula

Most doulas in the UK are private and paid. Some charities and volunteer schemes offer free or low-cost doulas.

Meet a few, and choose someone you feel relaxed with. Ask about their training, experience and backup plans.

When to call your midwife

  • A doula supports you, but your midwife is the person to call for any medical worry

Maternity units are open day and night. Calling is never a waste of anyone’s time.

Based on the Cochrane review of continuous support for women during childbirth. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Sources: Cochrane review: Continuous support for women during childbirth · Doula UK

Good to know

Is a doula a midwife?

No. A midwife gives medical care. A doula gives emotional and practical support.

Can my partner do this?

Yes, partly. Many people have both a partner and a doula.

Can I have a doula and a partner?

Yes. A doula supports both of you.

Will my hospital allow a doula?

Most do. Check your unit’s birth partner rules.

Do doulas help with caesareans?

Many do, and can support you before and after.
