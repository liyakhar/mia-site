First trimester: what to expect

The first trimester can feel big and quiet at the same time. Here is what is common, what helps, and when to call someone.

Summary

  • Common symptoms. Tiredness, nausea, tender breasts and needing to pee more are very common.
  • Nausea. Small, regular snacks and sips of water can help. If you cannot keep anything down, call your midwife or doctor.
  • Your first scan. The dating scan is offered between 11 and 14 weeks.
Call your midwife if
  • Bleeding from your vagina
  • You cannot keep food or fluids down for 24 hours
  • Very dark urine, or no pee for more than 8 hours
  • Tummy pain, a high temperature, or you vomit blood
  • You feel very weak, dizzy or faint

What is happening

In week 4 your baby is about 2 mm long, protected by a sac of fluid. By week 8 they are about the size of a raspberry, and by week 12 about the size of a plum.NHSNHSNHS

Around week 12 the placenta takes over feeding your baby from the yolk sac, and hormones often calm down a little.NHS

How you may feel

You may have no symptoms at first. Others are a missed period, a metallic taste, sore breasts, tiredness, new likes and dislikes for food, a stronger sense of smell and needing to pee more.NHSNHS

Light spotting can happen, but see a doctor if you have bleeding in pregnancy.NHS

If this is your first pregnancy you might not start showing until at least week 12.NHS

Morning sickness

Nausea and vomiting in early pregnancy is very common. It can happen at any time of day and usually clears up by weeks 16 to 20. It does not put your baby at increased risk.NHS

Things to try: rest, dry toast or a plain biscuit in the morning, small frequent meals of plain foods, cold foods if smells bother you, and sipping fluids. Ginger and acupressure bands may help.NHS

A severe form is called hyperemesis gravidarum. Call your midwife, GP or 111 if you cannot keep food or fluids down for 24 hours, have very dark urine or have not peed in 8 hours, feel faint, have tummy pain or a high temperature, or vomit blood.NHS

Looking after yourself

You may feel anxious or stressed at times. Rest as much as you can, eat small healthy meals, and share your worries with your midwife or doctor.NHS

Try to follow a healthy diet. You do not need to eat more for the baby in the first trimester.NHS

Your first appointment

Refer yourself to NHS maternity services as soon as you know you are pregnant. Your first midwife appointment is usually at 8 to 12 weeks and can take about an hour.NHS

Your midwife asks about your health, checks your blood pressure and urine, offers a blood test for your blood group, and gives you your maternity notes.NHS

Between 11 and 14 weeks you are offered a dating scan. It checks when your baby is due and whether you are having more than one baby. Screening tests at this scan are your choice.NHS

Good to know

When should I see a midwife?

As soon as you know you are pregnant, refer yourself to NHS maternity services. Your first midwife appointment is usually at 8 to 12 weeks.

Is morning sickness harmful to my baby?

No. It is unpleasant, but it does not put your baby at increased risk. Call your midwife or GP if you cannot keep fluids down.
