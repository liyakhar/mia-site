Home, birth centre or hospital: where to give birth

Summary

01

At home

With a midwife present. For healthy, low-risk pregnancies it is a safe option. Transfer to hospital is possible if needed.

02

Midwife-led unit

A homelike setting with midwives. Medical help is nearby or on site.

03

Hospital

Best if you or baby need extra care. Doctors and pain relief such as epidural are available.

Ask your midwife

Your history matters. Ask what is safest and what is available near you.

Your choice

In the UK you can choose where to give birth: at home, in a midwife-led unit, or in a hospital labour ward. Your choice may depend on your health and pregnancy.

Home birth

Two midwives come to your home. For people with healthy, low-risk pregnancies who have had a baby before, it is as safe for babies as a hospital birth, with fewer interventions.

For first babies, home birth carries a small extra risk for the baby, and about four in ten first-time mothers transfer to hospital, usually for slow labour, not emergencies.

Midwife-led units

Birth centres are homelike, run by midwives, and often have pools. For low-risk pregnancies they are safe for babies and have fewer interventions than labour wards.

Hospital labour ward

Best if you or your baby need extra care. Doctors, epidurals and theatres are on hand.

How to decide

Talk with your midwife about your health and what is near you. Visit your options if you can, and think about what helps you feel safe.

Good to know

Can I change my mind about where to give birth?

Yes. You can change your plan at any time, including in labour.

What if I need to transfer?

Your midwife will arrange it. Most transfers are not emergencies.

Is home birth safe for a first baby?

There is a small extra risk for first babies. Talk it through with your midwife.
