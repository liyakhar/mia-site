Baby movements in pregnancy

Your baby’s movements tell you they are doing well. Learn their usual pattern, and what to do if it changes.

Summary

  • When you feel them. Many people feel the first movements between weeks 16 and 24. It may be later in a first pregnancy.
  • Know your baby’s pattern. There is no set number of kicks. Get to know what is usual for your baby.
  • Less movement. If your baby moves less than usual, call your midwife or maternity unit straight away. Do not wait until the next day.
  • No home dopplers. Do not use a home doppler to check on your baby. Hearing a heartbeat does not mean your baby is well. Call your midwife.
Call your midwife if
  • Your baby is moving less than usual
  • The pattern of movements changes
  • You cannot feel your baby move any more. Call straight away
  • You have not felt your baby move by week 24

Why movements matter

If your baby is not well, they may be less active than usual. Less movement can be a sign of a problem, and the sooner it is found the better.NHS

Feeling your baby move is a reassurance that they are well. A reduction or a change in the pattern is what matters.RCOG

When you will feel them

Most people feel their baby move between 16 and 24 weeks. If this is your first baby, you might not feel movements until after 20 weeks.NHSRCOG

Movements can feel like a gentle swirling or fluttering, then kicks, swishes, rolls and jerks as your baby grows.NHSRCOG

If you have not felt your baby move by 24 weeks, tell your midwife. They will check your baby’s heartbeat and movements.NHSRCOG

What is normal

There is no set number of movements. Every baby is different, and you do not need to count kicks. What matters is getting to know your own baby’s usual pattern from day to day.NHS

Afternoon and evening are often the busiest times. Your baby also has sleep periods, mostly 20 to 40 minutes and rarely longer than 90 minutes, when they usually do not move.RCOG

The number of movements tends to go up until about 32 weeks, then stays about the same. You should feel your baby move right up to and during labour.RCOG

Why you may notice less

You are less likely to notice movements when you are busy or active. A placenta at the front of your womb can also make movements harder to feel.RCOG

Strong pain relief or sedatives, alcohol and smoking can make a baby move less. Sometimes a baby moves less because they are unwell.RCOG

If movements change

Call your midwife or maternity unit straight away if your baby is moving less than usual, you cannot feel them any more, or the pattern changes. Do not wait until the next day, even in the middle of the night.NHSRCOG

Never go to sleep ignoring a change in your baby’s movements. Call each time it happens, even if everything was fine before.RCOG

Do not use a home doppler or heartbeat kit. It is not a reliable way to check on your baby, and hearing a heartbeat does not mean your baby is well.NHSRCOG

There is not enough evidence to recommend a movement chart as routine. Knowing your baby’s own pattern matters more.RCOG

What care to expect

After 28 weeks you must contact your midwife or maternity unit immediately. You will be asked about the movements and checked, and your baby’s heartbeat will be listened to.RCOG

Between 24 and 28 weeks, call your midwife or unit. They will check your baby’s heartbeat and give you a full check-up. A scan may be arranged.RCOG

Good to know

Do I need to count kicks?

No. There is no set number. Get to know your baby’s usual pattern and call if it changes or slows down.

Do babies move less near the end of pregnancy?

No. The number of movements tends to stay about the same after 32 weeks, and you should feel your baby right up to and during labour.

What if I called before and everything was fine?

Call again each time. Midwives would rather check than have you wait.
