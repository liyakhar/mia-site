Safe sleep for newborns

Safe sleep is simple once you know it. Mia gives you the short list so you can rest more easily.

Summary

  • On the back. Always put baby to sleep on their back, for every sleep.
  • Own space. A firm, flat mattress in a cot or Moses basket in your room for the first six months.
  • Keep it clear. No pillows, bumpers, loose blankets or soft toys in the sleep space.
  • Avoid risk. Do not sleep with baby on a sofa or armchair, and do not smoke around baby.
Call your midwife if
  • Your baby is floppy, hard to wake or has trouble breathing. Call 999
  • Your baby has a fever or is unusually sleepy

Back to sleep

Always put your baby on their back to sleep, for every sleep, day and night. This is the most important thing you can do to lower the risk of sudden infant death.

A clear, flat space

Use a firm, flat, waterproof mattress in a cot or Moses basket. No pillows, duvets, bumpers or soft toys.

Place your baby with their feet at the foot of the cot, so they cannot wriggle under covers.

Same room

Keep your baby in a cot in your room for at least the first six months.

The right temperature

A room temperature of 16 to 20 degrees is ideal. Do not use hats indoors, and check your baby’s chest or back to see if they are too hot.

Things to avoid

Never sleep with your baby on a sofa or armchair. Do not smoke during pregnancy or around your baby.

If you share a bed, do it safely. Do not share if you or your partner smoke, have drunk alcohol, or taken drugs or sedating medicines.

Sources

  1. The Lullaby Trust: Safer sleep advice Lullaby Trust
  2. NHS: Reduce the risk of sudden infant death syndrome NHS

Good to know

Is it safe to share a bed?

The safest place is a separate cot in your room. Read the risks, especially if you smoke, drink or take sedating medicine.

How warm should the room be?

Between 16 and 20 degrees is advised. Dress baby lightly.

Can my baby sleep in a sling or car seat?

Not for long periods. Move them to a flat surface for sleep.
