Signs of labour and when to call your midwife

Labour often starts slowly. This guide covers the early signs, what real contractions feel like and when to call your midwife, so you are not left guessing.

Summary
  • Early signsRegular tightenings, a show (a pink or bloody mucus plug), backache or a watery trickle can all be early signs.
  • Real or practice?Practice contractions (Braxton Hicks) are irregular and ease with rest. Real contractions get longer, stronger and closer together.
  • When to callCall your midwife or maternity unit if your waters break, if you bleed, if baby moves less, or if contractions are regular and strong.
  • At home firstMany people stay at home in early labour: eat lightly, drink, rest and move. Your midwife will guide you on when to come in.
01

The signs

Labour may be starting if you have contractions or tightenings, a “show”, backache, an urge to go to the toilet, or your waters break.1

An urge to go to the toilet comes from your baby’s head pressing on your bowel.1

02

Contractions

In a contraction your womb tightens, then relaxes. For some people it feels like extreme period pain. Put your hand on your bump and you will feel it get hard, then ease.1

Labour contractions get longer, stronger and more frequent as labour goes on. Practice contractions (Braxton Hicks) are usually painless, short, infrequent and do not build up.1

03

A show

During pregnancy a plug of mucus sits in your cervix. It comes away just before or in early labour. It is sticky, jelly-like and pink because it holds a little blood.1

It may come in one blob or in pieces. Labour may follow quickly or take a few days, and some people have no show. If you lose more blood than that, call your midwife or hospital straight away.1

04

Your waters breaking

Your waters will most likely break during labour, but they can break before it starts. You may feel a slow trickle or a sudden gush.1

Amniotic fluid is clear and pale. Use a period pad, not a tampon, so your midwife can check the colour. Tell your midwife straight away if the fluid is smelly or coloured, or if you are losing blood.1

Most people go into labour within 24 hours of their waters breaking. If you do not, you will be offered an induction because there is a small increased risk of infection.1

05

The early stage

The start of labour is called the latent phase. Your cervix softens and thins, and starts to open. It can take hours, or sometimes days, and you will probably be advised to stay at home.12

Stay upright and gently active in the day, and rest at night if you can. Eat and drink. Breathing, massage, a warm bath or shower and paracetamol can help.12

Labour is called established when your cervix is about 4 cm open and contractions are stronger and more regular.2

06

When to call

Call your midwife or maternity unit if you think you are in labour, or if contractions come every 5 minutes or more often. You can also call if you are unsure or worried about anything.12

Call urgently if your waters break, you have vaginal bleeding, your baby moves less, you are under 37 weeks, a contraction lasts longer than 2 minutes, or you have 6 or more contractions in 10 minutes. Do not wait until morning.12

Call 999 if you think your baby is coming now and you have a strong urge to push. If you cannot reach your midwife or unit, call 111.12

Call
Call your midwife if
  • Contractions every 5 minutes or closer
  • Your waters break
  • Bleeding from your vagina
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • Under 37 weeks and you think you are in labour
  • A contraction lasts over 2 minutes, or 6 or more in 10 minutes
Good to know
How will I know it is real labour?

Real contractions get longer, stronger and closer together, and do not ease when you rest or change position. If you are unsure, call your midwife or maternity unit.

Should I go to hospital straight away?

Not usually. In early labour you will probably be advised to stay at home. Call first and they will tell you when to come in.

What if my waters break but there are no contractions?

Call your midwife. Most people go into labour within 24 hours. If you do not, you will be offered an induction.
