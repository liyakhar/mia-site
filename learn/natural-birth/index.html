Natural birth: what helps and what the evidence says

A natural birth starts and carries on by itself, with only the medical help you need. This guide covers what tends to help, how strong the evidence is and what your other options look like.

Summary
  • Move and stay uprightWalking, swaying and kneeling in early labour can help labour move along. Research links upright positions with a shorter first stage.
  • Continuous supportHaving one person with you the whole time, like a doula or partner, is linked with fewer interventions and a better experience.
  • Eat, drink and restIn early labour, eat and drink, rest at night if you can, and stay gently active by day.
  • Every option is finePain relief, induction or a caesarean can be the right choice. Mia explains them too, without judgement.
01

What natural birth means

Most births in England, around 6 in 10, are vaginal births without help.1

There are 3 stages of labour. In the first, contractions open your cervix to about 10 cm. In the second, your baby is born. In the third, the placenta comes out.23

02

The first stage

The early, or latent, phase can take many hours or even days. Eat and drink, rest at night if you can, and stay upright and gently active in the day.23

Established labour starts when your cervix is about 4 cm open and contractions are stronger and more regular. In a first pregnancy the time to full dilation is usually 8 to 18 hours. It is often quicker, around 5 to 12 hours, in a second or third pregnancy.2

03

The second stage

Your midwife helps you find a position to give birth in. You may feel an urge to push that feels like needing to poo.2

Pushing should last no longer than 3 hours with a first baby, or 2 hours if you have had one before.2

The third stage, when the placenta comes out, is usually over within 30 minutes. You are offered an injection of oxytocin to help.14

04

Staying upright and moving

Moving around, staying upright and changing position can mean a shorter labour, less pain, fewer interventions and more sense of control.5

Birth balls, kneeling, leaning on your partner and a warm bath or shower are all worth trying.35

05

Coping with pain

Learn about labour, practise breathing and relaxation, keep moving, and bring a support person. Paracetamol and a warm bath can help early on.6

Breathing, visualisation and relaxation, as used in hypnobirthing, may help you feel more relaxed and in control.7

Keep an open mind. You may want more pain relief than you planned, and that is fine. Gas and air, injections, an epidural and a birth pool are all options depending on where you give birth.6

06

Support

People who have continuous support in labour are less likely to have an assisted birth, a caesarean, pain relief or a longer labour.8

Choose a birth partner you trust, and talk through your birth plan and wishes beforehand.8

Call
Call your midwife if
  • Contractions every 5 minutes or more often
  • Your waters break, or you have vaginal bleeding
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • You are under 37 weeks and think you are in labour
Good to know
How long is labour?

In a first pregnancy, established labour to full dilation usually takes 8 to 18 hours, and often 5 to 12 in a later pregnancy. The early phase can take much longer.

Do I have to avoid pain relief for a natural birth?

No. Keep an open mind. You can use pain relief and still have a vaginal birth.
