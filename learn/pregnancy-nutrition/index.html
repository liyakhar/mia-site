What to eat in pregnancy and what to avoid

You do not need to eat for two. This guide covers what helps you and your baby, and which foods to avoid, without the stress.

Summary
  • Eat a varied dietFruit, vegetables, wholegrains, protein and dairy or alternatives. You do not need to eat for two.
  • Key supplementsIn the UK, folic acid until week 12, and vitamin D from October to March, or all year if you get little sun.
  • Foods to avoidAvoid liver, raw or undercooked meat, raw fish and shellfish, and unpasteurised dairy. Limit tuna and oily fish.
  • Caffeine and alcoholKeep caffeine under 200 mg a day, about two mugs of instant coffee. The safest choice with alcohol is none.
01

Eating well

You do not need a special diet. Eat a variety of foods every day to get the nutrients you and your baby need.1

You do not need to “eat for 2”, even with twins or triplets. You may be hungrier than usual, but a healthy breakfast can help you avoid snacking on foods high in fat and sugar.1

You do not need to get the balance right at every meal, but try to get it right over a week.1

02

What to put on your plate

Aim for at least 5 portions of fruit and vegetables a day. Fresh, frozen, canned, dried or juiced all count. Wash fresh produce carefully.1

Starchy foods such as bread, potatoes, rice and pasta should make up just over a third of what you eat. Choose wholegrain or higher-fibre options where you can.1

Eat some protein every day: beans, pulses, fish, eggs, poultry, lean meat and nuts. Cook poultry, burgers, sausages and whole cuts of meat until steaming hot with no pink.1

Eat 2 portions of fish a week, 1 of them oily, such as salmon, sardines or mackerel. Dairy foods give you calcium. Choose low-fat versions, or unsweetened calcium-fortified alternatives.1

03

Vitamins

Take 400 micrograms of folic acid every day, ideally from 3 months before you conceive until the end of week 12. It lowers the chance of your baby having a neural tube defect such as spina bifida.2

Some people need a higher 5 mg dose, which a GP can prescribe. This includes people with diabetes, a blood condition such as sickle cell, or who take medicine for epilepsy or HIV.2

Take 10 micrograms of vitamin D every day from early October to late March. Some people need it all year, for example if you cover most of your skin outdoors or spend a lot of time indoors.2

Your iron is checked at your first appointment and around 28 weeks. Do not take cod liver oil or supplements with vitamin A. Too much can harm your baby’s development.2

04

Foods to avoid

Avoid raw or undercooked meat, liver and liver products, game meats such as pheasant and venison, unpasteurised milk and dairy, and mould-ripened soft cheeses like brie and camembert unless cooked until steaming hot.3

Avoid raw fish and raw shellfish, and swordfish, shark and marlin. Cold-smoked fish such as smoked salmon should be cooked until steaming hot.3

Eggs with the British Lion stamp are safe raw or runny. Other hen eggs should be cooked until the white and yolk are firm.3

Listeria bacteria can cause miscarriage, stillbirth or a very unwell newborn. That is why chilled ready-to-eat foods need care. Keep them cold and eat them before the use-by date.3

05

Drinks

Have no more than 200 mg of caffeine a day. That is about 2 mugs of instant coffee. A cup of tea has about 75 mg and a can of cola about 40 mg. Too much caffeine is linked to a higher chance of miscarriage and stillbirth.3

Avoid alcohol. It can cause long-term harm to your baby and it is safest to avoid it. Limit herbal teas to 4 cups a day and avoid teas with liquorice root.3

Limit tuna to 4 cans or 2 steaks a week, and oily fish to 2 portions a week. They can contain pollutants such as mercury.3

06

If you have already eaten something on the list

If you ate a small amount of something you should avoid and you did not get ill, it is unlikely to affect your baby. Talk to your midwife or GP if you are worried.3

Get help from 111 if you feel unwell after eating or drinking something that should be avoided, have vomiting or diarrhoea for more than 48 hours, or have signs of listeriosis or toxoplasmosis.3

Call
Call your midwife if
  • You feel unwell after eating something on the avoid list. Call 111
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea for more than 48 hours. Call 111
  • You are very tired and may be low in iron. Ask your midwife for a blood test
Good to know
Do I need to eat for two?

No. Even with twins or triplets you do not need to eat for two. Eat a healthy, varied diet.

Which vitamins do I need?

Folic acid every day until week 12, and vitamin D from October to March, or all year if you get little sun. Avoid supplements with vitamin A.

Can I have coffee?

Yes, up to 200 mg of caffeine a day. That is about 2 mugs of instant coffee. Tea, cola, energy drinks and chocolate also count.
