Pregnancy week by week

Each week brings small changes for you and your baby. Mia gives you one clear view of your week: how big your baby is, what your body is doing and one thing worth doing now.

Summary

  • Weeks 1 to 13. The first trimester. Tiredness and nausea are common. Your first scan is usually around week 12.
  • Weeks 14 to 27. The second trimester. Many people feel more energy. You may feel the first movements.
  • Weeks 28 to 40. The third trimester. Baby grows fast. From week 24, notice your baby’s usual pattern of movements.
  • After 40 weeks. Many babies arrive after their due date. Your midwife will talk with you about your options.
Call your midwife if
  • Your baby moves less than usual, or the pattern changes
  • You have bleeding from your vagina
  • Fluid leaks from your vagina
  • You have a bad headache, problems with your eyes, or sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet
  • You have strong tummy pain that does not go away
  • You have itching, especially on your hands and feet, later in pregnancy

How pregnancy weeks are counted

Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last period, not from the day you conceived. That is why you are already “four weeks pregnant” when a test first turns positive. A full-term pregnancy is around 40 weeks, and anywhere from 37 to 42 weeks is normal.

Your due date is a best guess. Only about one baby in twenty arrives on the exact day. Your dating scan, usually between weeks 10 and 14, gives the most reliable date.

The first trimester: weeks 1 to 13

Your baby’s heart, brain and spine begin to form in these weeks. By week 12 your baby is fully formed and about the size of a plum, though still very small.

For many people this is the hardest stretch. Tiredness, nausea, sore breasts and big feelings are common, even though nobody can see your bump yet. Rest when you can and keep meals small and simple.

The second trimester: weeks 14 to 27

Energy often comes back and nausea fades. Your bump starts to show, and somewhere between weeks 16 and 24 you will likely feel the first flutters of movement.

The anomaly scan, offered at 18 to 21 weeks, checks how your baby is growing. This is also a good time to start thinking about where you want to give birth and to book antenatal classes.

The third trimester: weeks 28 to 40

Your baby grows quickly, puts on fat and usually turns head down. You may feel heavier, more breathless and more tired again. Sleeping on your side from week 28 is advised.

From now on, get to know your baby’s usual pattern of movements. Pack your bag around week 36, finish your birth plan and talk with your midwife about the signs of labour.

Your check-ups

In the UK, a first pregnancy usually has around ten antenatal appointments, and later pregnancies around seven. Your midwife checks your blood pressure, urine and the growth of your bump, and talks you through tests and choices.

Bring your questions. Writing them down between visits helps. No question is too small.

Good to know

When should I call my midwife?

If your baby moves less than usual, if you bleed, or if something feels wrong. Do not wait for your next visit.

Does Mia replace my check-ups?

No. Mia helps you understand your week. Your midwife and doctor look after you.

How is my due date worked out?

It is counted as 40 weeks from the first day of your last period, then usually adjusted at your dating scan.

How many scans will I have?

Most people in the UK are offered two: a dating scan at 10 to 14 weeks and an anomaly scan at 18 to 21 weeks. You may be offered more if needed.

Is it normal not to feel pregnant?

Yes. Symptoms come and go, and some people have very few. If you are worried, talk to your midwife.
