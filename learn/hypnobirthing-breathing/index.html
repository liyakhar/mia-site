Breathing and calm for labour

Slow, steady breathing can help you cope with contractions. These are simple techniques you can practise at home in a few minutes a day.

Start free
01

Slow breathing

Breathe in through your nose, then out slowly through your mouth, longer than the in-breath.

02

Relaxation

Soften your jaw, shoulders and hands. A relaxed body often means a calmer mind.

03

Practise often

A few minutes a day in pregnancy makes it easier to use when labour comes.

04

The evidence

Studies suggest relaxation-based preparation can reduce fear of birth. It does not replace pain relief if you want it.

Based on Cochrane reviews of relaxation and antenatal education, and NHS guidance on coping with labour. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Good to know

Is hypnobirthing the same as no pain?

No. It helps with fear and tension. Pain relief is still your choice.

When should I start practising?

Many people start around week 28, but any time helps.
