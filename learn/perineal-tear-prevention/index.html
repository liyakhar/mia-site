Perineal massage and tear prevention

Many people worry about tearing. Mia explains what the research says helps and how to talk about it with your midwife.

Tears are common

The perineum is the skin between your vagina and anus. Many people have a small tear or graze in a vaginal birth, especially with a first baby. Most heal well.

A smaller number have a deeper tear that involves the muscle around the back passage. These need repair in theatre and extra care afterwards.

Perineal massage

From about week 34 to 35, gentle massage of the perineum a few times a week may lower the chance of tears that need stitches and of an episiotomy, especially in a first birth.

Use clean hands and a plain oil. Press gently down and to the sides for a few minutes. Your partner can help.

In labour

A warm compress held on the perineum as your baby crowns is linked with fewer serious tears.

Slow, controlled pushing as the head is born, guided by your midwife, also helps.

Positions

Upright, side-lying or all-fours positions may help. Talk to your midwife about what suits you.

After a tear

Keep the area clean and dry, change pads often, and pour warm water over it when you pee. Rest, take pain relief and drink plenty.

Pelvic floor exercises help healing. Ask for a referral to a physio if needed.

When to call your midwife

  • Pain that gets worse
  • The wound smells bad or has pus
  • Fever
  • You cannot control wind or poo

Maternity units are open day and night. Calling is never a waste of anyone’s time.

Based on Cochrane review of perineal massage and RCOG guidance on tears. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Sources: Cochrane review: Antenatal perineal massage · RCOG: Perineal tears during childbirth · Cochrane review: Perineal techniques during the second stage of labour

Good to know

Does everybody tear?

No. Many people have no tear, or a small one that heals easily.

Is an episiotomy common?

It is used only when needed. Talk to your midwife about when it might be offered.

When can I start massage?

Usually from around week 34 to 35.
