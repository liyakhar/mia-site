Braxton Hicks contractions or real labour?

Practice tightenings can feel strong and worrying. Mia helps you tell them apart from true labour.

What Braxton Hicks are

Braxton Hicks are practice contractions. Your womb tightens for a short time, then relaxes. They help your womb get ready for labour.

Some people feel them from the second trimester. They often become more noticeable in the last weeks.

What they feel like

Your bump goes hard and tight for up to a minute or two. They are usually irregular and not very painful, more uncomfortable.

They often come after activity, a full bladder, sex, or when you are dehydrated.

What helps

Change position. If you are sitting, walk, and if you are moving, rest. A warm bath, a glass of water and slow breathing help too.

Use them to practise your breathing for labour.

How real labour is different

Real contractions come regularly and get longer, stronger and closer together over time. They do not ease when you rest or change position.

You may also notice a show, your waters breaking or backache. If you are not sure, call your midwife or maternity unit.

When to call your midwife

  • Regular or painful contractions before 37 weeks
  • Bleeding from your vagina
  • Fluid leaking
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • Constant tummy pain that does not ease

Maternity units are open day and night. Calling is never a waste of anyone’s time.

Based on NHS guidance on Braxton Hicks and signs of labour. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Good to know

When do they start?

Some people feel them from the second trimester. They are more common later.

Can they hurt?

They can feel uncomfortable. Strong pain that does not ease needs a call to your midwife.

Does everyone get them?

Most people do, but not everyone notices them.
