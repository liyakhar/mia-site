Natural birth, explained honestly

A natural birth is a labour that starts and carries on by itself, with only the medical help you need. Here is what tends to help, how strong the evidence is, and what your other options look like.

What natural birth means

Natural birth usually means labour that starts on its own and moves on with as little medical help as is safe for you and your baby. It often means birth without an epidural, using movement, water and support to cope.

It is not a test you pass or fail. Many births include some help along the way, and that is still your birth.

Why your hormones matter

Labour is driven by hormones, mainly oxytocin. Oxytocin works best when you feel safe, warm, private and unhurried. Fear and stress hormones can slow labour down.

That is why dim light, quiet voices, a familiar person and freedom to move can make a real difference.

Move and stay upright

Walking, swaying, kneeling and leaning forward use gravity and help your baby move down. A Cochrane review found upright positions in the first stage are linked with a shorter labour and fewer epidurals.

A birth ball, a pool or a partner to lean on all help. Change position whenever your body asks.

Continuous support

Having one person with you the whole way through, such as a partner, friend or doula, is one of the best-proven ways to help labour. It is linked with fewer interventions and a better birth experience.

Talk with your support person before labour about what helps you and what you want.

Getting ready in pregnancy

Antenatal classes, breathing practice and learning the stages of labour all help you feel calm and ready.

Write down your wishes, and talk them through with your midwife. Think about where you want to give birth, since midwife-led units and home births have fewer interventions for people with healthy pregnancies.

Every option is fine

Pain relief, induction or a caesarean can be the right choice for you or your baby. Mia explains these fairly too.

If plans change, you can still keep things that matter to you, like skin-to-skin, dim lights or your music.

When to call your midwife

  • Your waters break, especially if the fluid is green, brown or smells
  • You have bleeding
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • Contractions are regular and strong, or you feel you need support
  • You feel unsure or worried. Calling is always fine

Maternity units are open day and night. Calling is never a waste of anyone’s time.

Based on Cochrane reviews of upright positions in labour and of continuous support in labour. Mia teaches. Your midwife knows you best.

Sources: Cochrane review: Maternal positions and mobility during first stage labour · Cochrane review: Continuous support for women during childbirth · NICE guideline NG235: Intrapartum care

Good to know

Is natural birth safer?

It depends on your health and your pregnancy. Talk it through with your midwife.

Can I change my mind in labour?

Yes. Your birth plan is a wish list, not a contract.

Does natural birth hurt more?

Labour is intense for most people. Movement, water, breathing and support can help a lot, and pain relief is always there if you want it.
