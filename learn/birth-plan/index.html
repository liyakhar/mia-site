How to write a birth plan

A birth plan is a short note about what matters to you in labour. Keep it to one page that your team can read in a minute.

Summary

  • Keep it short. One page is enough. Use plain words and put your most important wishes first.
  • What to include. Who you want with you, positions and movement, pain relief wishes, and your feeding plan.
  • Plan for change. Birth can go a different way. Add what you would want if plans change, such as skin-to-skin after a caesarean.
  • Share it early. Talk it through with your midwife before labour and bring a copy.

What a birth plan is

A birth plan is a written record of what you would like to happen in labour and after the birth. It can also include things you want to avoid.Tommy’s

You do not need one. But writing one can help you understand your choices even if you do not use it in the end.Tommy’s

How to make one

You do not need a special form. A few points on a sheet of paper is fine, or you can use a template from the NHS. Put a few copies in your hospital bag.Tommy’s

Your midwife can answer questions, tell you about options in your area and help you work out what you want.Tommy’s

What to include

Who you want as your birth partner, where you want to give birth, positions you would like to try, and what pain relief you want.Tommy’s

Whether you want delayed cord clamping, skin-to-skin contact straight away, and how you plan to feed your baby.Tommy’s

How you feel about an episiotomy, ventouse or forceps, and fetal monitoring. Any special needs, like an interpreter or religious customs.Tommy’s

Share it with your birth partner so they can support you better.Tommy’s

If you are having a caesarean

You can still have a plan. You may want the screen lowered to see your baby’s birth, or a quiet theatre so your voice is the first your baby hears.Tommy’s

If you are not planning a caesarean, it may still be worth adding a section in case you need one. Unless it is a real emergency there should be time to follow your choices.Tommy’s

After the birth

You will be offered vitamin K for your baby, to help prevent a rare bleeding disorder. You can say in your plan if you would prefer it by mouth, which needs more doses.Tommy’sNHS

Delayed cord clamping should happen automatically, but it can help to mention it.Tommy’s

Staying flexible

Things do not always go to plan. A pool may not be free, or you may need to move to hospital during a home birth. Do not worry about covering every possibility.Tommy’s

You can change your mind at any time, even in labour. Keep an open mind, because you may want more pain relief than you planned.Tommy’sNHS

Good to know

Do I have to write a birth plan?

No. There is no need unless you want one.

Can I change it during labour?

Yes. You can change your mind at any time, including during labour.
