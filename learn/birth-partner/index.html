Birth partner guide: how to help in labour

A good birth partner helps you feel safe and heard. This guide covers what to do and what to say, in calm, plain steps.

Summary
  • Know the planRead the birth plan together. You can speak up for the wishes when your partner is busy coping.
  • Comfort measuresOffer water, massage, a cool cloth, a change of position and calm words.
  • Stay calmSlow breathing from you helps. It is fine to ask the midwife to explain what is happening.
  • Look after yourselfEat, drink and take short breaks. You will be needed after the birth too.
01

Choosing who

A birth partner gives practical and emotional support in labour. Having someone with you can help you cope better with contractions and help labour go more smoothly.1

It can be your baby’s parent, your partner, a close friend or relative, or a doula. Choose the person you trust most and who you feel will support you best. Do not worry about hurting feelings.1

02

What makes a good one

Someone calm under pressure who is sensitive to your needs, can help you move around, explains what is going on clearly, and supports your breathing and relaxation.1

They should be able to ask for help or speak up for you, and take control if you need them to.1

03

How many

You can have more than one. At home you can have as many as you like. In hospital or a birth centre there may be a limit, and there may be one for a caesarean too. Ask your midwife in pregnancy.1

A carer or interpreter should not count as a birth partner.1

04

Getting ready together

Talk well in advance about how you want them to help. Share your hopes and fears, show them your birth plan, and show them any relaxation methods or TENS pads you plan to use.1

Antenatal classes with your birth partner can help you both feel prepared.1

Remind them you can change your mind at any point. Only you can consent to treatment, but they can pass on your wishes to staff if you need them to.1

05

After the birth

In hospital there may be a limit on how long a birth partner can stay. Some can stay overnight, others must leave and return in visiting hours.1

If you are worried about your partner leaving, talk to a nurse on your ward and explain why. They may be able to arrange for them to stay longer.1

Good to know
Can I have more than one birth partner?

Yes. At home there is no limit. In hospital or a birth centre there may be one, so ask your midwife.

Can my partner stay after the birth?

It depends on your hospital’s policy. Ask a nurse on the ward if you want them to stay longer.
