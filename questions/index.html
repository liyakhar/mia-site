Questions you might be asking.

Short answers based on NHS, WHO and Cochrane guidance. For your own situation, ask your midwife.

When should I call my midwife?

If your baby moves less than usual, if you bleed, or if something feels wrong. Do not wait for your next visit. Read more: Pregnancy week by week

Does Mia replace my check-ups?

No. Mia helps you understand your week. Your midwife and doctor look after you. Read more: Pregnancy week by week

Is natural birth safer?

It depends on your health and your pregnancy. Talk it through with your midwife. Read more: Natural birth

Can I change my mind in labour?

Yes. Your birth plan is a wish list, not a contract. Read more: Natural birth

Can I have pain relief in the pool?

Gas and air, yes. For an epidural you leave the pool. Read more: Water birth

Do I have to give birth in the water?

No. Many people labour in the pool and give birth outside it. Read more: Water birth

Is a doula a midwife?

No. A midwife gives medical care. A doula gives emotional and practical support. Read more: Doulas

Can my partner do this?

Yes, partly. Many people have both a partner and a doula. Read more: Doulas

When should I get help for my mood?

If you feel low for more than two weeks, or have scary thoughts, call your midwife or doctor now. Read more: After birth

Is skin-to-skin possible after a caesarean?

Often, yes. Ask your team about it before the birth. Read more: After birth

What if my waters break?

Call your midwife or maternity unit. Note the time and the colour of the fluid. Read more: Signs of labour

How far apart are contractions when I should go in?

Your midwife will tell you what suits you. Many units advise calling when they come regularly every 5 minutes or sooner. Read more: Signs of labour

Is a birth plan a promise?

No. It is a wish list. Your team will keep you and your baby safe first. Read more: Birth plan

Who should see my birth plan?

Your midwife, your birth partner and anyone supporting you in labour. Read more: Birth plan

Should I drink something cold to wake my baby?

No. This is a myth. If movements are reduced, call your midwife. Read more: Baby movements

Do babies move less near the end?

No. Babies keep moving up to and during labour. Read more: Baby movements

Is hypnobirthing the same as no pain?

No. It helps with fear and tension. Pain relief is still your choice. Read more: Breathing for labour

When should I start practising?

Many people start around week 28, but any time helps. Read more: Breathing for labour

What should I take in early pregnancy?

In the UK, 400 micrograms of folic acid daily is advised before pregnancy and until week 12. Ask your midwife what suits you. Read more: First trimester

Is spotting normal?

Light spotting can be common but always tell your midwife. Call at once if bleeding is heavy or with pain. Read more: First trimester

Can I move with a monitor or drip?

Often, yes. Ask your midwife which positions work with your equipment. Read more: Labour positions

Is lying on my back bad?

It is not unsafe for everyone, but upright and side positions are linked with a shorter first stage. Read more: Labour positions

Can I change my mind about pain relief?

Yes, at any time. It is your choice. Read more: Pain relief

Does an epidural harm my baby?

It is generally considered safe. Your anaesthetist can talk through the risks with you. Read more: Pain relief

Can I say no to induction?

Yes. It is your decision. Your team will explain the risks and benefits so you can choose. Read more: Induction

Does a membrane sweep hurt?

It can feel uncomfortable or crampy for a short time. Read more: Induction

Can I have skin-to-skin after a caesarean?

Often, yes. Ask your team before the birth. Read more: Caesarean birth

Can I have a vaginal birth after a caesarean?

Many people can. Talk to your midwife or doctor early in pregnancy. Read more: Caesarean birth

What if I choose to bottle feed?

That is a valid choice. Mia explains bottle feeding safely too. Read more: Breastfeeding basics

Is it normal for feeding to hurt at first?

A little tenderness can be common. Ongoing pain is not, so ask for help with the latch. Read more: Breastfeeding basics

When can I exercise again?

Gentle walking is fine early on. Ask your midwife or doctor before harder exercise, often after the 6 week check. Read more: Postnatal recovery

When is the 6 week check?

Usually between 6 and 8 weeks after birth. Your GP will check you. Read more: Postnatal recovery

Do I need to eat for two?

No. You need about 200 extra calories a day only in the last three months. Read more: Eating in pregnancy

Is it safe to eat fish?

Yes, and one or two portions a week is good. Avoid shark, swordfish and marlin, and limit oily fish. Read more: Eating in pregnancy

Can I keep running?

Many people can if they were runners before. Listen to your body and talk to your midwife. Read more: Exercise in pregnancy

Is exercise bad for the baby?

No. For most healthy pregnancies, regular activity is safe and linked with benefits. Read more: Exercise in pregnancy

Do I need to bring my own pillow?

Many people like to. Check your unit’s advice. Read more: Hospital bag

What should my birth partner bring?

Snacks, a charger, cash for parking and a change of clothes. Read more: Hospital bag

Can I change my mind about where to give birth?

Yes. You can change your plan at any time, including in labour. Read more: Where to give birth

What if I need to transfer?

Your midwife will arrange it. Most transfers are not emergencies. Read more: Where to give birth

What if I feel faint at the birth?

Sit down and tell the midwife. It is common and nothing to be embarrassed about. Read more: Birth partner

Should I speak up for my partner?

Yes, politely, about wishes you have talked through. Ask questions rather than argue. Read more: Birth partner

Does everybody tear?

No. Many people have no tear, or a small one that heals easily. Read more: Perineal care

Is an episiotomy common?

It is used only when needed. Talk to your midwife about when it might be offered. Read more: Perineal care

Can I do this after a caesarean?

Often, yes. Ask your team about it before the birth. Read more: Cord clamping

Does waiting raise any risk?

For healthy babies, waiting at least a minute is recommended. Your team will advise if there is a concern. Read more: Cord clamping

Is it safe to share a bed?

The safest place is a separate cot in your room. Read the risks, especially if you smoke, drink or take sedating medicine. Read more: Safe sleep

How warm should the room be?

Between 16 and 20 degrees is advised. Dress baby lightly. Read more: Safe sleep

Is it my fault?

No. It is an illness, not a failure, and it is treatable. Read more: Postnatal mood

Will treatment affect breastfeeding?

Some treatments are fine while breastfeeding. Your doctor can advise. Read more: Postnatal mood

When do they start?

Some people feel them from the second trimester. They are more common later. Read more: Braxton Hicks

Can they hurt?

They can feel uncomfortable. Strong pain that does not ease needs a call to your midwife. Read more: Braxton Hicks

Can I travel in the second trimester?

Often yes, and many find it the easiest time. Check with your midwife first. Read more: Second trimester

Should I sleep on my side?

From the third trimester, going to sleep on your side is advised. Do not worry if you wake on your back. Read more: Second trimester

Is it normal to feel more tired?

Yes. Rest when you can and accept help. Read more: Third trimester

Why is sleeping on my side advised?

Research links going to sleep on the back in late pregnancy with a higher risk of stillbirth. Side sleeping is safer. Read more: Third trimester

How many dates do people eat?

Studies often used about six dates a day, from week 36. Read more: Dates from week 36

Can dates start labour?

There is no proof they start labour. Some small studies link them with a readier cervix and less need for induction. Read more: Dates from week 36
